# Deck narrative

## The arc: diagnosis → proof → cure → action

Every IdaProject talk, whatever the topic, follows the same arc. Slide counts below are for a 45–60 minute talk. Scale the body chapters for shorter slots, but keep the opening and closing blocks whole: they carry credibility and recall.

| Block | Slides | Job | Typical types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 3 | who speaks, why trust them, what the talk claims | Cover, Trust, Thesis cards |
| Chapter 1: the stakes | divider + 3–5 | why this matters to this audience, in money and consequences | Divider, Scheme, Cards, Formula table |
| Chapter 2: the problem | divider + overview + series | break the problem into a finite list of causes | Overview cards, Series, Myths, Hero numbers |
| Chapter 3: the model / cure | divider + series | the framework, one rule or level per slide | Series, Levels, Comparison table |
| Chapter 4: how to adopt | divider + 2–4 | where it fits in the company's process, who owns it, the key mistake | Timeline, Owner cards, Error rows |
| Closing | 4–5 | close the loop and give homework | Manifesto, Roadmap, Thesis cards (repeat), Homework, CTA |

A product pitch replaces chapters 3–4 with a product cycle: problem stat → product title slide in the product's brand colour → results in numbers → case with screenshots → punchline → QR link.

## Rules that make the arc work

1. **Fix the idea and three theses first.** They appear on slide 3 and, word for word, near the end. Repeating them is what lets an audience recall the talk a week later.
2. **Open chapters with dividers.** A divider is 2–5 words on a full-bleed neon background. Phrase it as intrigue or a question ("Почему нет результата?"), not a rubric ("Проблемы"), so it pulls the audience into the next part.
3. **Overview, then series.** When a chapter has N items of the same kind, first show all N on one overview slide, then give each its own slide on an identical template.
   - Each series slide carries a kicker such as «ОШИБКА 2» or «ПРАВИЛО 5».
   - Where items connect across chapters, show the link top-right ("решается шагом 3"). This stitches the problem to its cure.
4. **One point per slide.** If a slide holds two tables or two conclusions, split it into two slides with the same kicker, and let the second title continue the first ("…и что с этим делать").
5. **Rhythm.** Every 3–6 dense slides, give the eye a rest: a divider, a manifesto line or one big number. Never put more than two tables in a row.
6. **A turn before the cure.** Before the solution chapter, a myth-busting or reality-check slide works well, for example struck-through misconceptions or "what the contract actually promises" with hard numbers. It creates the need the cure answers.
7. **End with practice.** Close with a "first 90 days" or "three steps" slide that marks which steps cost nothing, then homework that names a concrete artefact and a deadline. "Start implementing" is not homework.
8. **A closing manifesto.** Before the practical slides, add 2–3 parallel lines on the neon background, for example "X отвечает за…, Y — за…, вы — за…". It is the sentence people quote.

## From a brief or script to slides

- Each paragraph of the script is a slide candidate. The slide shows the paragraph's **conclusion**, not a retelling. Lead-ins such as "now three minutes about…" stay in the speaker's mouth.
- **Numbers** each become a visible element: a big figure, a table cell or a badge. Flag numbers that have no source.
- **Lists** of 3–5 items become cards or chips. Lists of 6–8 become table rows. More than 8 should be grouped or turned into a series.
- **Comparisons** (before/after, convenient/risky) become two columns with a neon `→` between them.
- **Processes** become a timeline with dots, or week columns with `→`. Put a bracket with a chip over a group of steps, for example "без бюджета".
- **Misconceptions** become struck-through quotes in «ёлочки», in grey.
- **Tiered models** (maturity levels, service tiers) get a levels slide showing what each tier is, a small diagram and two key metrics. Then add tables of consequences or prices per tier.
- **Things the audience fills in** (their own numbers, an action plan) become dashed input fields with a pencil icon. A bare dash reads as unfinished work.

## Titles and copy

- **Titles** are 2–7 words: a claim, a question or a formula ("Сайт — это не витрина, а отдел продаж").
- **The conclusion line** of a slide is one or two lines. Strong patterns are "не X, а Y" and "X — это Y". Emphasise with colour (grey → white → neon), not bold.
- **Card copy** is a 2–4 word uppercase heading plus a 6–20 word lowercase explanation, with no full stop.
- **Voice** is expert, direct and a little provocative. Industry terms stay; vivid metaphors belong in conclusions and theses, not in card labels.
